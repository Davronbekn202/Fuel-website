How makes fuel

1. Crude Oil → Fuel (Petroleum-Based) Most fuels come from crude oil, a mixture of hydrocarbons extracted from underground. The process mainly happens in refineries: Step 1: Distillation Crude oil is heated in a large furnace. It goes into a distillation tower. Components separate by boiling point: Light fuels like propane and butane rise to the top. Gasoline comes from the middle. Diesel, kerosene, and heavier oils are lower. Step 2: Conversion Some fractions are chemically converted to make more gasoline or diesel. Methods include: Cracking – breaking large molecules into smaller ones. Reforming – rearranging molecules to increase octane. Step 3: Treatment & Blending Remove impurities like sulfur. Add additives to improve engine performance or prevent corrosion. Blend different fractions to make: Gasoline Diesel Jet fuel Heating oil 2. Biofuels Made from plants, waste, or algae. Common biofuels: Ethanol – from corn, sugarcane. Biodiesel – from vegetable oils or animal fats. Process: Fermentation (for ethanol) – sugars → ethanol + CO₂. Transesterification (for biodiesel) – fats/oils + alcohol → biodiesel + glycerin. Can be blended with regular fuel (e.g., E10 = 10% ethanol + 90% gasoline). 3. Synthetic Fuels (Fischer-Tropsch) Made from natural gas, coal, or biomass. Converts syngas (CO + H₂) into liquid fuels. Mostly used when crude oil is scarce or for specialized fuels. Key Points: Refineries are huge chemical factories. Fuel quality is controlled by standards (octane, sulfur content). Blending with biofuel reduces pollution and dependency on oil.
